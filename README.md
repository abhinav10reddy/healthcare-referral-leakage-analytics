# Healthcare Referral Leakage Analytics

When a patient is referred to a specialist, the referral can stall before an appointment
happens: the patient is never scheduled, cancels or misses the visit, or is sent out of
network and never returns. This project measures that "leakage" end to end on a synthetic
dataset of 147,750 referrals. It runs a Python and SQLite pipeline with reconciled KPIs
and a three-page Power BI dashboard. The analysis is aimed at referral coordinators,
operations analysts and service-line managers who want to see where referrals are lost
and how long each step takes.

> **Synthetic data notice**
>
> Every record in this project is fictional and generated by a seeded random-number
> generator. It contains no real patient, provider or facility information, and no
> external healthcare data. Facility and provider names are invented. The project is for
> portfolio and educational use only and must not be used for clinical decision-making
> or real operational decisions.

## Main features

- **Reproducible synthetic data.** A seeded generator creates referrals, patients,
  providers, facilities and appointments. It adds controlled data-quality problems:
  duplicates, missing fields and inconsistent closures. The same seed always gives
  byte-identical files.
- **Validation and cleaning.** 35 structural checks run before any data is loaded into
  SQLite. Every cleaning action is logged as a data-quality exception rather than
  silently fixed.
- **SQL analytics layer.** SQLite views follow each referral from contact to scheduling
  to outcome. They classify every referral as Converted, Active or Leaked, and give each
  leaked referral a single primary reason.
- **KPI reconciliation.** 26 automated checks confirm that the SQL results agree with
  the base tables, with grouped totals, and with an independent Python calculation.
- **Power BI data package.** The pipeline writes a validated CSV snapshot, a
  star-schema model specification, 20 DAX measures, a theme, and a file of expected
  values to check the dashboard against.
- **Power BI dashboard.** Three report pages cover referral outcomes, leakage by
  provider and service, turnaround times, and data quality.
- **Safe reruns.** Each stage writes to a staging folder. It replaces the previous
  outputs only after every check passes.

A few patterns in the synthetic data:

- **Network status:** referrals to the two out-of-network facilities convert at about
  54%, compared with 70–78% for in-network facilities. All of their leaked referrals are

- **Service category:** leakage is highest in Behavioral Health (29.9%) and lowest in

- **Referral source:** Emergency Department referrals leak most (30.6%), and Specialist

  with 26 for Routine referrals.
- **Recent months:** December 2025 converts at only 57.3%, because 1,754 of its
  referrals are still active at the extract date. This is why active referrals are kept
  separate from leakage.

## KPI definitions and classification rules

The definitions are implemented in
[sql/02_create_analytics_views.sql](sql/02_create_analytics_views.sql). They are
documented in full in [sql/KPI_DEFINITIONS.md](sql/KPI_DEFINITIONS.md), which is the
reference for every rule below.

**Reporting window.** Referral dates fall in 2025. Follow-up activity is recorded up to
the extract date, 2026-01-31, and the follow-up window is 30 days.

**Classification.** Every referral gets exactly one outcome:

- **Converted.** The referral has at least one completed appointment, **or** it was sent
  to an external facility and closed with a confirmed external visit.
- **Active.** The referral is not converted, but is still legitimately open at the
  extract date. That means one of these holds:
  - it has a scheduled appointment after the extract date;
  - it is pending with no appointment and is less than 30 days old;
  - it is pending, its last appointment was cancelled or missed less than 30 days before
    the extract date, and it is awaiting rebooking;
  - it was sent externally less than 30 days ago, so an outcome may still arrive.

  An open status alone does not make a referral active.
- **Leaked.** The referral is neither converted nor active, and it meets a leakage rule.
  The rules are checked in priority order, and the first match becomes its single
  primary leakage reason:

  | Priority | Primary leakage reason | Rule |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1 | External Outcome Missing | Sent externally, no confirmed outcome, and the 30-day follow-up window has passed |
  | 2 | Expired Before Completion | Referral status is Expired |
  | 3 | Cancelled Without Successful Rebooking | The last appointment was cancelled |
  | 4 | No-Show Without Successful Follow-Up | The last appointment was a no-show |
  | 5 | Declined Before Scheduling | Referral status is Declined |
  | 6 | Not Scheduled Within 30 Days | No appointment at all, 30 or more days after the referral date |

- **Unclassified.** A referral that fits none of the three groups is reported as
  Unclassified rather than forced into one. The seed-42 data has none.

Conversion rate and leakage rate both divide by total referrals. They do not add up to
100%, because active referrals are neither.

**Other measures:**

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Scheduled referral | At least one appointment record, of any status |
| Completed referral | At least one completed appointment |
| Scheduling rate / completion rate | Scheduled or completed referrals divided by total referrals |
| Delayed scheduling | First booking more than 30 days after the referral date |
| Delayed appointment | First appointment later than the service category's illustrative target (10–30 days) |
| Referrals with cancellations / no-shows | Referrals with at least one cancelled or no-show appointment, each counted once |
| Turnaround times | Whole days from the referral date to first contact, first booking and first appointment. Missing dates are excluded, never treated as zero |
| Data-quality exceptions | One row per issue found and logged during cleaning; a referral can have more than one |

Counts are at referral level unless a name says *appointment*. Appointments are
aggregated per referral before joining, so a referral with several appointments is never
counted twice.

## Project workflow

```
generate_data.py  ──>  prepare_data.py  ──>  analyze_data.py  ──>  prepare_powerbi_data.py  ──>  Power BI Desktop
 raw CSV files         validation,           SQL views, 26          validated snapshot and        dashboard (.pbix)
                       cleaning, SQLite      reconciliation         expected KPIs
                                             checks, exports
```

1. **Generate** ([generate_data.py](generate_data.py)). Writes six raw CSV files:
   - 150,000 referral rows, including deliberate duplicates;
   - 60,000 patients and 128,718 appointments;
   - 132 providers at 12 facilities;
   - 8 service categories.

   It also adds controlled data-quality issues at fixed rates set in
   [config.py](config.py).
2. **Prepare** ([prepare_data.py](prepare_data.py)). Validates the raw files and stops
   on any structural error. It then cleans the data and loads seven tables into SQLite
   using [sql/01_create_schema.sql](sql/01_create_schema.sql). Each cleaning action is
   logged as an exception:

   | Data-quality issue | Exceptions | Handling |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Duplicate referral record | 2,250 | Duplicate row removed |
   | Missing referral source | 2,955 | Set to `Unknown` |
   | Missing first-contact date | 2,216 | Kept blank; no date invented |
   | Missing receiving facility | 1,478 | Inferred from the provider |
   | Missing closed date | 1,478 | Kept blank |
   | Open referral with a closed date | 739 | Closed date cleared |

3. **Analyze** ([analyze_data.py](analyze_data.py)). Creates the views in
   [sql/02_create_analytics_views.sql](sql/02_create_analytics_views.sql). It then runs
   26 reconciliation checks and writes the CSV exports and a JSON summary.
4. **Snapshot** ([prepare_powerbi_data.py](prepare_powerbi_data.py)). Exports the six
   tables the dashboard uses and writes `expected_kpis.json` from the SQL views. It
   validates both with 12 checks before replacing the tracked copies.
5. **Report.** The dashboard was built by hand in Power BI Desktop from the snapshot,
   following the build guide. No script creates or edits the `.pbix`.

## Getting started

You need Windows, Python 3.12 and Git. Power BI Desktop is needed only to open the
report. Run these commands in Windows PowerShell:

```powershell
git clone https://github.com/abhinav10reddy/healthcare-referral-leakage-analytics.git
cd healthcare-referral-leakage-analytics
py -3.12 -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r requirements.txt
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe generate_data.py --referrals 150000 --seed 42
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe prepare_data.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe analyze_data.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe prepare_powerbi_data.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pytest -q
```

- **Order matters.** Each script after the generator checks that the previous stage
  succeeded, and stops with the command to run if it did not.
- **Timing.** The four pipeline steps take a few minutes in total; most of it is the
  analytics step, about 100 seconds. The test suite takes about 4 minutes.

| Step | Output | Tracked by Git? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `generate_data.py` | `data\raw\` – six raw CSV files | No (ignored; recreate at any time) |
| `prepare_data.py` | `data\processed\` – six cleaned CSV files and `data_quality_exceptions.csv` | No (ignored) |
| `prepare_data.py` | `data\output\healthcare_referrals.db` (SQLite) and `phase2_data_quality.json` | No (ignored) |
| `analyze_data.py` | `data\output\` – 12 CSV exports and `phase3_analytics_summary.json`; adds the views to the database | No (ignored) |
| `prepare_powerbi_data.py` | `powerbi\data\` – six CSV files, and `powerbi\expected_kpis.json` | Yes |
| `pytest` | Temporary folders only | No |

**The Power BI snapshot is tracked, so the report works straight after cloning.** Running
the pipeline with seed 42 rebuilds byte-identical snapshot files, so `git status` stays
clean.

## How to use and manually test the project

1. **Run the pipeline.** Use the commands in [Getting started](#getting-started). Each
   script prints row counts and check results. `analyze_data.py` ends with the headline
   KPIs, which should match [Key results](#key-results-seed-42).
2. **Inspect the JSON reports:**
   - `data\output\phase2_data_quality.json` should show `"pipeline_status": "succeeded"`
     and `"checks_failed": 0` under `validation`.
   - `data\output\phase3_analytics_summary.json` should show
     `"analytics_status": "succeeded"` and `"checks_failed": 0` under `reconciliation`.
3. **Inspect the CSV outputs:**
   - Open `data\output\overall_kpis.csv` or `data\output\leakage_reason_metrics.csv` in
     any text editor.
   - `data\output\referral_journey.csv` has one row per referral, with every flag and
     the primary leakage reason.
4. **Open the SQLite database.** Open `data\output\healthcare_referrals.db` in any SQLite
   viewer, or query it from the project's Python:

   ```powershell
   .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -c "import sqlite3; c = sqlite3.connect('data/output/healthcare_referrals.db'); print(c.execute('SELECT total_referrals, converted_referrals, leaked_referrals, active_referrals FROM vw_overall_kpis').fetchone())"
   ```

   This prints `(147750, 106864, 38594, 2292)`. Every view is described in
   [sql/KPI_DEFINITIONS.md](sql/KPI_DEFINITIONS.md#views).
5. **Open the report in Power BI Desktop.** Open
   `powerbi\healthcare_referral_leakage_dashboard.pbix`.
   - The data is imported into the file, so the pages show values without a refresh.
   - To refresh from the CSV files after cloning to a different folder, point each data
     source at `powerbi\data\` first: **Transform data > Data source settings > Change
     Source**.
6. **Use the slicers.** Each slicer filters only its own page. Two checks to try:
   - On Referral Overview, select *Cardiology* under Service Category. The Provider list
     shortens to Cardiology providers, and the cards show 23,684 referrals, 74.3%
     conversion and 24.6% leakage.
   - Select *Behavioral Health Provider 108*. This provider is at an out-of-network
     facility, so the scheduling medians show blank rather than 0.
7. **Compare the cards with the expected values.** With every slicer cleared, each card
   should match its `display` value under `kpis` in
   [powerbi/expected_kpis.json](powerbi/expected_kpis.json). For example,
   `conversion_rate` is `72.3%`. The file also holds the expected values by month,
   service category, referral source, urgency and insurance type.
8. **Review the dashboard without Power BI.** The three screenshots in
   [screenshots/](screenshots/) show every page. They can be viewed on GitHub or in any
   image viewer.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data:** distributions, rates, wait times and appointment targets are
  illustrative generator settings. They do not describe any real healthcare system.
- **Analytical conventions:** the leakage rules and the 30-day window are conventions
  for this synthetic model, not clinical or contractual standards.
- **Receiving side only:** the data records only the receiving provider and facility.
  Referrals are analyzed by referral source, not by referring clinician.
- **External referrals:** these have no appointment detail. Their outcome comes only
  from a confirmed-visit closure reason.
- **What the tests cannot check in the report:** the report's model settings, such as
  data types, relationships and DAX text, are stored in a binary data model that the
  tests cannot read. The verification checklist lists these as not proven.
- **Interactions:** slicer interactions are documented with expected results, but they
  are tested by hand.
- **Repository history:** the Power BI snapshot (about 25 MB) and the report (about
  3 MB) are tracked. Each regenerated snapshot or re-saved report adds to the Git
  history.
